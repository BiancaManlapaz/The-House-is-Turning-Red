The man proclaims the house belongs to him. You argue that you and your family still live here. He is your guest. He tells you to look around.

There are no more children in your house. They have all left for a school the man promised would teach them. Although, it feels less like they have left and more like they have been taken. You don’t know where the children are.

Your house has been stripped of its decor. The baskets and masks that were woven and sculpted by your mother and sister are gone. There are no rugs, no blankets, and you realize that for a long time, this house has been empty.

Your sister and her children are dead. Your parents are sick, something that carried over from the winter. Your brother had left home ages ago to fight in a war he never came back from. His promised land, rights, and privileges are left unfulfilled.

And so the man paints your house red. He paints over the walls your family built, over the marks left by wear and tear, and over the smoke stains from the kitchen stove. He fills the halls and rooms with decorations of his own. He invites his brothers over for dinner, and he laughs at the dinner table while sharing your food.

You find yourself alone in a house you no longer recognize.
